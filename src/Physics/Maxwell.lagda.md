# Electromagnetism, synthetically {defines="maxwell-field-strength gauge-invariance"}

The paper's account of classical field theory singles out
electromagnetism as the test case for the *differential* column of
the probe table: a vector potential $A$, a gauge redundancy in its
choice, and a field strength $F = \mathrm{d}A$ that is gauge
*invariant* — provably, not by appeal to a computation with indices.
Every ingredient is already assembled: the potential is a [[Kähler
differential|kahler-differentials]] $1$-form on the [[polynomial
ring|polynomial-ring]] of coordinate functions on the plane, and the
field strength lives in the [[Kähler 2-forms|kahler-2-forms]] just
built, with its `d¹`{.Agda} the algebraic exterior derivative.

```agda
module electromagnetism {ℓ} (R : CRing ℓ) where
  open Algebra.Ring.Polynomial R
  open Algebra.Ring.Kahler R
  open Algebra.Ring.Kahler.Exterior R
```

The plane has two coordinate functions $x, y$, the free generators of
the polynomial ring on two variables.

```agda
  x̂ ŷ : ⌞ Plane ⌟
  x̂ = var (lift fzero)
  ŷ = var (lift (fsuc fzero))
```

## The vector potential, in Landau gauge

A constant magnetic field in the plane admits the **Landau gauge**
potential $A = x\,\mathrm{d}y$ — asymmetric in $x$ and $y$, favouring
one direction, exactly the choice a physicist makes when they "pick a
gauge."

```agda
  A-landau : Ω¹ Plane con-hom
  A-landau = x̂ ·ω dₖ ŷ
```

## The field strength

Applying the exterior derivative computes the field strength: the
Leibniz rule expands $\mathrm{d}(x\,\mathrm{d}y)$ into
$\mathrm{d}x\wedge\mathrm{d}y + x\,\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{d}y)$, and the
second term dies since $\mathrm{d}\circ\mathrm{d} = 0$, leaving the
constant field $F = \mathrm{d}x\wedge\mathrm{d}y$.

```agda
  F : Ω² Plane con-hom
  F = d¹ A-landau

  F-value : F ≡ x̂ d∧d ŷ
  F-value =
    F                                        ≡⟨⟩
    wedge x̂ (dₖ ŷ) +² (x̂ ·² d¹ (dₖ ŷ))       ≡⟨ ap (wedge x̂ (dₖ ŷ) +²_) (ap (x̂ ·²_) (d¹-d ŷ) ∙ ·²-absorb x̂) ⟩
    wedge x̂ (dₖ ŷ) +² 0²                     ≡⟨ +²-idr (wedge x̂ (dₖ ŷ)) ⟩
    wedge x̂ (dₖ ŷ)                           ≡⟨⟩
    x̂ d∧d ŷ                                  ∎
```

A constant magnetic field: $F = \mathrm{d}x \wedge \mathrm{d}y = B$,
the constant $2$-form generated by the two coordinates — computed,
not postulated.

## Gauge equivalence

The potential is not unique: adding the differential of any function
$\chi$ — here $\chi = xy$ — changes $A$ but leaves the field strength
untouched. This is precisely the `gauge`{.Agda} invariance proved for
the abstract exterior derivative, instantiated at the Landau
potential.

```agda
  A' : Ω¹ Plane con-hom
  A' = A-landau +ω dₖ (x̂ *ₚ ŷ)

  same-field : d¹ A' ≡ F
  same-field = gauge A-landau (x̂ *ₚ ŷ)
```

The two potentials $A$ and $A'$ describe the same physics: they
differ by a gauge transformation, and the field strength — the only
gauge-invariant, physically meaningful quantity — is identical,
*proved* rather than checked component by component.
