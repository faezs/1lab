# The harmonic oscillator, constructively {defines="harmonic-oscillator"}

The [[reading guide|higher-topos-theory-in-physics]] closes with the claim that the
formalized stack is a *constructive simulator*: one syntax, all
semantics. This module cashes the claim on the hydrogen atom of all
of physics — the harmonic oscillator — by pushing the *same* system
through each column of the paper's probe table that we have built:
its dynamics are **compiled and run** (and its conservation laws
*proven*, not observed); its force law is **derived** from the
potential by synthetic differentiation; its fermionic partner obeys
the **Pauli exclusion principle** as a theorem of ring algebra; and
its discrete symmetry is **gauged**, with the homotopy quotient
remembering exactly the stabilizer that the naive quotient forgets.

## Dynamics, compiled and conserved

The oscillator's phase space has a coordinate and a momentum, both
valued in the single base type of a [[λ-signature]]; the dynamics is
generated by two operations. Working in units where the mass and
stiffness are one and time advances by a quarter period, the exact
stroboscopic evolution is the symplectic rotation $(x, p) \mapsto
(p, -x)$: the coordinate *drifts* to where the momentum points, and
the momentum receives the Hooke-law *kick*.

```agda
data Osc-type : Type where
  coord : Osc-type

data Osc-op : types.Ty Osc-type → Osc-type → Type where
  drift : Osc-op (` coord `× ` coord) coord
  kick  : Osc-op (` coord) coord
```

The evolution law is a *program* — a term of the simply-typed λ
calculus over the signature — and therefore makes sense in every
[[cartesian closed category]] at once.

```agda
step-program : Expr (∅ , ` coord `× ` coord) (` coord `× ` coord)
step-program =
  `⟨ `hom drift (`var stop) , `hom kick (`π₁ (`var stop)) ⟩
```

To *simulate*, interpret the signature in sets — the coordinate
becomes the integers, drift reads off the momentum, and the kick is
Hooke's law, a restoring force linear in displacement.

```agda
private
  module SetsModel = Cat.CartesianClosed.Free.Model Oscillator
    (Sets-cartesian {ℓ = lzero}) Sets-closed

oscillator-interp
  : elim.base-method
      SetsModel.chaotic-cartesian SetsModel.chaotic-closed
      (λ _ → el! Int)
oscillator-interp drift = λ (x , p) → p
oscillator-interp kick  = λ x → negℤ x

private
  module Run = SetsModel.model (λ _ → el! Int) oscillator-interp

step : Int × Int → Int × Int
step s = Run.compile .F₁ ⟦ step-program ⟧ᵉ (lift tt , s)
```

The compiled evolution *computes*: released at $x = 3$ with momentum
$4$, the oscillator traces its orbit — and returns home after four
quarter-periods — definitionally.

```agda
_ : step (3 , 4) ≡ (4 , -3)
_ = refl

_ : step (4 , -3) ≡ (-3 , -4)
_ = refl

_ : step (step (step (step (3 , 4)))) ≡ (3 , 4)
_ = refl
```

Better: the orbit closes for *every* initial condition. Discrete
time translation is an exact $\mathbb{Z}/4$ symmetry of the
simulator, not an artifact of the sample point.

```agda
period-four : ∀ s → step (step (step (step s))) ≡ s
period-four (x , p) i = negℤ-negℤ x i , negℤ-negℤ p i
```

And the Noether charge of that time-translation symmetry — the
energy $E = x^2 + p^2$ — is conserved by the flow, again for every
state, again as a theorem rather than a numerical observation.

```agda
energy : Int × Int → Int
energy (x , p) = x *ℤ x +ℤ p *ℤ p
```

## The force, derived from the potential

The `kick`{.Agda} above was an *input* — Hooke's law put in by hand.
The infinitesimal column of the probe table lets us instead *derive*
it: over any [[commutative ring|commutative-ring]] of coefficients,
the potential $V = x^2$ lives in the [[polynomial
ring|polynomial-ring]] in one variable, and its derivative is
computed synthetically, by evaluating $V$ at the thickened point $x
+ \epsilon$ of the [[dual numbers|dual-numbers]] and reading off the
coefficient of $\epsilon$ — the Kock–Lawvere recipe, with the
[[thickened site|formal-smooth-set]] guaranteeing this is the honest
tangent calculus.

```agda
module force-from-potential {ℓ} (R : CRing ℓ) where
  open Algebra.Ring.Polynomial R
  open Algebra.Ring.Kahler R
```

<!--
```agda
  private
    R[x] : CRing ℓ
    R[x] = R[ Lift ℓ (Fin 1) ]

    module R' = CRing-on (R .snd)
    module Rx = CRing-on (R[x] .snd)
    module CR = Cat.Reasoning (CRings ℓ)
```
-->

```agda
  x̂ : ⌞ R[x] ⌟
  x̂ = var (lift fzero)

  V : ⌞ R[x] ⌟
  V = x̂ *ₚ x̂

  taylor : CR.Hom R[x] (Dual.R[ε] R[x])
  taylor = extend (Dual.ι-dual R[x] CR.∘ con-hom) (λ _ → x̂ , con R'.1r)

  δ : ⌞ R[x] ⌟ → ⌞ R[x] ⌟
  δ p = taylor .∫Hom.fst p .snd

  hooke : δ V ≡ x̂ +ₚ x̂
  hooke = ap₂ _+ₚ_ Rx.*-idr (*ₚ-idl x̂)
```

The restoring force $F = -\delta V = -2x$ is linear in the
displacement: Hooke's law is a *computation*, two lines from the
Leibniz rule that the dual-number multiplication carries in its
$\epsilon$-component. The same derivative, packaged invariantly, is
a [[Kähler differential|kahler-differentials]] — the potential's
field strength as a 1-form, the object classified by the
(non-concrete!) [[de Rham classifier|de-rham-classifier]]:

```agda
  dV : Ω¹ R[x] con-hom
  dV = dₖ V

  dV≡2x·dx : dV ≡ (x̂ +ₚ x̂) ·ω dₖ x̂
  dV≡2x·dx = d-leibniz x̂ x̂ ∙ sym (·ω-distr x̂ x̂ (dₖ x̂))
```

Constant potentials exert no force — the paper's statement that the
de Rham differential kills the constants is the constructor
`d-const`{.Agda} itself.

## The fermionic oscillator

The supersymmetric partner of the oscillator replaces its bosonic
coordinate by anticommuting ones: fermionic creation operators
$\psi_i = \theta_i$ in the [[Grassmann algebra|grassmann-algebra]]
over the same coefficients. The **Pauli exclusion principle** — no
two fermions in the same mode — is a generating relation of the
algebra:

```agda
module fermionic {ℓ} (R : CRing ℓ) (q : Nat) where
  open Algebra.Ring.Grassmann R q
```

<!--
```agda
  private
    module R' = CRing-on (R .snd)
    module Λr = Algebra.Ring.Reasoning Λ[q]
```
-->

```agda
  pauli-exclusion : ∀ i → θ i *g θ i ≡ con R'.0r
  pauli-exclusion = θ-sq
```

More is true, and this needs proof: a *dressed* mode — any linear
combination $a\psi_i + b\psi_j$ of creation operators — is again
fermionic, i.e. squares to zero. This is the defining computation of
the CAR algebra: the cross terms cancel by anticommutativity, the
diagonal terms die by exclusion.

Fermion-number parity $(-1)^F$ — the superselection grading of the
fermionic sector — is the [[parity involution|grassmann-algebra]]:
odd modes flip sign, definitionally, and applying it twice is the
identity.

  _ = σ-σ

## Gauging the parity symmetry

The oscillator has a discrete symmetry: negating both coordinate and
momentum preserves the energy (it is `step`{.Agda} applied twice).
Physics demands this symmetry be *gauged* — configurations related
by it are the same physical state — but forbids taking the naive
quotient, which would discard the symmetry data. The
[[action groupoid|action-groupoid]] is the correct object. First,
the symmetry group and its action on phase space:

```agda
Flip : Group lzero
Flip = to-group mk where
  mk : make-group Bool
  mk .make-group.group-is-set = hlevel 2
  mk .make-group.unit = false
  mk .make-group.mul = xor
  mk .make-group.inv x = x
  mk .make-group.assoc = xor-associative
  mk .make-group.invl = xor-inverse-self
  mk .make-group.idl x = refl

Phase : Set lzero
Phase = el! (Int × Int)
```

<!--
```agda
private
  module Sl = Cat.Reasoning (Sets lzero)

  negate : Int × Int → Int × Int
  negate (x , p) = negℤ x , negℤ p

  negate-negate : ∀ s → negate (negate s) ≡ s
  negate-negate (x , p) i = negℤ-negℤ x i , negℤ-negℤ p i

  flip-phase : Phase Sl.≅ Phase
  flip-phase = Sl.make-iso negate negate
    (funext negate-negate) (funext negate-negate)

  flips : Bool → Phase Sl.≅ Phase
  flips false = Sl.id-iso
  flips true  = flip-phase
```

```agda
parity-action : Action (Sets lzero) Flip Phase
parity-action .fst = flips
parity-action .snd .is-group-hom.pres-⋆ = pres where
  pres : ∀ x y → flips (xor x y) ≡ (Aut (Sets lzero) Phase .snd Group-on.⋆ flips x) (flips y)
  pres false y    = Sl.≅-path refl
  pres true false = Sl.≅-path refl
  pres true true  = Sl.≅-path (funext λ s → sym (negate-negate s))
```

The parity symmetry preserves the Hamiltonian, so it is a symmetry
of the *dynamics*, fit to be gauged:

```agda
parity-preserves-energy : ∀ s → energy (negate s) ≡ energy s
parity-preserves-energy (x , p) =
  ap₂ _+ℤ_ (square-negℤ x) (square-negℤ p)
```

The gauged phase space is the homotopy quotient — as a simplicial
set, the [[nerve]] of the action groupoid — and its plots are
exactly the physics: vertices are configurations, edges are
configurations *together with* the gauge transformation acting on
them.

```agda
module Gauged =
  Cat.Instances.SimplicialSets.ActionGroupoid Flip Phase parity-action

gauge-states : ⌞ Gauged.homotopy-quotient .F₀ 0 ⌟ ≃ (Int × Int)
gauge-states = Gauged.quotient-vertices

gauge-histories
  : ⌞ Gauged.homotopy-quotient .F₀ 1 ⌟ ≃ ((Int × Int) × Bool)
gauge-histories = Gauged.quotient-edges
```

Finally, the payoff of using the groupoid: the origin of phase space
is a *fixed point* of parity, and the homotopy quotient remembers
its stabilizer. There are two genuinely different gauge loops at the
origin — the identity, and the parity flip that happens to fix it —
where the quotient *set* would have collapsed them.

```agda
origin : Int × Int
origin = 0 , 0

identity-loop flip-loop : Gauged.Action-groupoid .Hom origin origin
identity-loop = false , refl
flip-loop     = true , refl

gauge-loops-differ : ¬ identity-loop ≡ flip-loop
gauge-loops-differ e = true≠false (sym (ap fst e))
```

This is the orbi-singularity of the paper's singular column, in its
smallest instance: the point $\mathrm{pt}/\!\!/(\mathbb{Z}/2)$
sitting inside the gauged phase space, invisible to sets, seen by
the topos.
